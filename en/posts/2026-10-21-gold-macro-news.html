A Qatar–Pakistan U.S.–Iran peace proposal sends gold up $22 to 4,153.50 – and the move fully unwinds after a diplomat says a deal isn't close

War & geopoliticsNeutralOct 20, 10:15 AM ET (report) → 10:30 AM ET (4,153.50 high) → 12:15 PM ET (gold back near 4,124)

The New Arab (Al-Araby Al-Jadeed): Qatar and Pakistan put a 'middle-ground' proposal to Iran and the U.S. – Iran would allow a confidential IAEA visit to its nuclear sites, and Washington would let Tehran collect interest on $6 billion in frozen funds held in Qatar; Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir delivered it in Tehran. Bloomberg: a diplomat involved in the talks said reports that a deal is close are inaccurate; gold swung between a 0.4% gain and a 0.7% loss. On CNBC's XAU= feed, spot ran from 4,130 to 4,153.50 in 15 minutes (10:15–10:30 AM ET), then settled back into 4,120–4,126. Meanwhile the IRGC said two tankers caught fire after hitting mines in the Strait of Hormuz, and Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia continued.

Gold impact: Near term: peace headlines pull oil and yields lower and gold pops – but without confirmation the move fades fast, so stay flat for the first 15–30 minutes after a headline. Longer term: a real peace deal would cut crude and inflation expectations and take hike pressure off the Fed – that's the big bullish catalyst for gold; a drawn-out war keeps oil above $100 and yields high.
