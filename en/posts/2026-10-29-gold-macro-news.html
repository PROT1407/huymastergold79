Gold macro brief, Oct 29, 2026 – Fed holds at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10–2 vote – Cook and Hammack dissent for a hike, Warsh won't pre-commit on December

Fed holds at 3.75%–4.00% on a 10–2 vote – Cook and Hammack dissent for a hike, Warsh won't pre-commit on December

FOMCBearish gold ↓Oct 28, 2:00 PM ET (statement) – presser 2:30 PM ET

The FOMC left the funds rate at 3.75%–4.00%, as expected, but Governor Lisa Cook and Cleveland Fed President Beth Hammack both dissented in favor of a quarter-point hike. The statement upgraded growth to a "strong pace," said inflation "remains elevated" and that the "accumulation of more information" will guide the next move. At the presser, Chair Kevin Warsh refused to prejudge December, saying the Fed has "made clear our readiness to act," and announced that one 2027 meeting will be strategy-only, with no rate vote. CNBC called Cook's dissent the real stunner – her first since joining the Board in 2022.

Gold impact: Near term: a hold with two hawkish dissents keeps a December hike priced at roughly 82%–86%, so gold lacks a catalyst to break out and rallies into 4,080–4,100 are likely to be sold. Longer term: a Fed still leaning toward tightening into a fast-growing economy keeps real rates high – the biggest headwind for bullion until inflation clearly rolls over.

Gold impact: Near term: Kitco maps resistance at 4,059 then 4,079–4,103, support at 4,039, 4,015 and 3,975 – a tight range into 8:30 AM ET GDP and PCE. A hot PCE firms up hike bets and puts $4,000 back on the table. Longer term: price is still below its key daily moving averages (FXStreet), so the intermediate trend leans lower until the 10-year backs off 5%.

Crude bounces back: Brent settles $115.32 (+1.3%), WTI $102.78 (+2.9%) after Houthi missile claim on Ras Tanura

OilBearish gold ↓Oct 28, ~2:30 PM ET (settlement)

CNBC/Reuters: Brent rose $1.44 to $115.32 a barrel and WTI gained $2.88 to $102.78. The Houthis claimed a ballistic missile strike on Saudi Arabia's 550,000-bpd Ras Tanura refinery – not yet confirmed by Riyadh – and attacked Aden airport in Yemen, while traders worry the US will resume strikes on Iran after next week's midterms. EIA data showed commercial crude stocks down 1.9 million barrels to 410.6 million, the lowest since July 31, and the SPR at its lowest since October 1982 (DTN).

Gold impact: Near term: Brent above $115 revives the inflation scare and backs up December hike pricing – a net negative for gold, even if the war premium props up some haven demand. Longer term: tight supply and an open-ended conflict keep oil elevated, which means sticky inflation and higher-for-longer rates – an intermediate-term headwind for bullion.

🔗 Source: CNBC – Oil rises as investors weigh a prolonged Middle East conflict · DTN – WTI back above $100 on Saudi attack, US inventory draws

10-year yield posts its biggest jump in a month to 5.044% while the 2-year slips to 4.731% – dollar eases to ~102

YieldsBearish gold ↓Oct 28, 4:01 PM ET (WSJ, close)

WSJ: the 10-year yield settled up 5.2 bp at 5.044%, its largest one-day rise in a month, while the 2-year finished little changed at 4.731% after dropping sharply during Warsh's press conference; the 30-year hit 5.36% (MarketWatch). The curve steepened as Warsh avoided forward guidance. CNBC: the dollar index fell 0.2% to 102.15 and the euro rose 0.3% to $1.1160. Stocks edged up, with the S&P 500 +0.28% at 7,743.98.

Gold impact: Near term: a softer dollar helps at the margin, but a 10-year above 5% is a lid on gold – a sustainable bounce needs yields back under 5%. Longer term: long-end yields near 24-year highs show investors demanding more compensation for inflation and deficit risk, keeping the opportunity cost of owning gold high.

🔗 Source: WSJ – Treasury yields mixed after Fed hold · CNBC – Dollar falls after Fed holds rates steady
