Gold macro brief, Oct 15, 2026 – September PPI runs double the forecast (+1.3% m/m, 6.3% y/y), retail sales +0.8% – gold dips for five minutes, then pops to 4,167

September PPI runs double the forecast (+1.3% m/m, 6.3% y/y), retail sales +0.8% – gold dips for five minutes, then pops to 4,167

InflationNeutralOct 15, 8:30 AM ET (BLS, Census); gold marks through 9:34 AM ET

BLS (8:30 AM ET): September PPI rose 1.3% m/m (est. 0.6%; prior revised to 0.5%) and 6.3% y/y, the biggest monthly and annual gains since 2022. Energy +10.8%, gasoline +20.3%; PPI ex food and energy +0.7% (est. 0.3%), ex food, energy and trade just +0.5%. Census: retail sales +0.8% (est. 0.3%), ex-autos +0.9% (est. 0.6%), but ex autos and gas only +0.25% after about 1.1% in August. CME FedWatch (via CNBC): October hike odds near 34%, up from under 18% a week ago. Spot gold (CNBC XAU=): 4,149.80 at the print, a 4,138.10 low within five minutes, a 4,167.10 high at 9:15 AM ET, and 4,156.20 at 9:30 AM ET.

Gold impact: Near term: hot data 'should' hit gold, but with energy doing most of the PPI damage and non-gas spending slowing, bonds caught a bid and yields fell, so gold bounced. If yields turn back up, that bounce is at risk. Longer term: 6.3% producer inflation tends to filter into consumer prices, which gives the Fed cover to hike again at the Oct. 27–28 meeting and keeps pressure on gold; the Iran war both feeds inflation and keeps haven demand alive.

Hormuz heats up: Singapore-flagged boxship sunk, IRGC 'counting the moments' to hit U.S. warships – reports Iran is evacuating bases

War & geopoliticsBullish gold ↑Oct 14, 8:40 PM ET (evacuation reports, unconfirmed) · 9:35 PM ET (CNBC Daily Open)

CNBC Daily Open (9:35 PM ET Oct 14): the Singapore-registered container ship GFS Pride was hit by an unidentified projectile, caught fire and sank in the Strait of Hormuz; all 21 crew were evacuated safely. A second vessel, GFS Giselle, took hull damage but reached safe anchorage. Singapore's maritime authority called the attacks a breach of international law. Tehran said it is 'counting the moments' until it can strike U.S. warships, while CENTCOM said U.S. and partner forces clearly control the strait. investingLive (8:40 PM ET Oct 14): unconfirmed reports say the IRGC ordered several military bases evacuated, and Iranian officials reportedly told regional diplomats they are '100% certain' a U.S. strike is coming.

Gold impact: Near term: rising U.S.–Iran escalation risk supports haven demand, especially if the evacuation reports are confirmed or a strike actually lands. The oil channel cuts the other way by feeding inflation and Fed hike bets, so gold benefits most when war headlines hit as a surprise. Longer term: a drawn-out conflict (the IMF now assumes Hormuz reopens only in March 2027) keeps a floor under haven and central bank demand.

Fed's Daly: more hikes likely needed, but no rush at the Oct 27–28 meeting – Chair Warsh speaks at the IMF in Bangkok late Thursday New York time

FedBearish gold ↓Oct 14, 5:44 PM ET (Daly); Warsh speaks late Thursday, Oct 15, New York time

investingLive (citing an exclusive Reuters interview, 5:44 PM ET Oct 14): San Francisco Fed President Mary Daly said further rate increases are more likely than not, but she is still weighing timing and sees no need to act at the Oct 27–28 meeting. She said gasoline is driving headline inflation while core remains sticky, upside inflation risks stay elevated, and a stable labor market lets the Fed focus on prices. Chicago Fed President Austan Goolsbee called the CPI data discouraging, and the Beige Book flagged intensifying, diesel-driven price pressure. Investing.com notes Chair Kevin Warsh speaks late Thursday New York time at the IMF meetings in Bangkok, before the Fed's blackout begins Saturday.

Gold impact: Near term: Daly takes some air out of an October hike (a small plus for gold) but confirms the tightening bias, firming up a December move – a net negative. Warsh is the next big event risk after PPI and retail sales. Longer term: 'higher for longer' keeps real yields positive, the main headwind for bullion.

🔗 Source: investingLive – Fed's Daly: more rate hikes likely needed, but no urgency to move in October · CNBC (Reuters) – Gold rebounds from one-week low as dollar slips

Fed's Bowman: time to wait and see how markets and the economy evolve after the September hike

FedNeutralCited in CNBC's Oct 14 post-session gold update

CNBC (post-CPI gold wrap Oct 14): Fed Vice Chair for Supervision Michelle Bowman said the central bank has time to wait and see how financial markets and the economy evolve after September's rate rise before deciding on further action. That 'no rush' tone softens part of the hawkish post-CPI impulse and fits the end-of-day FedWatch pullback in October hike odds from the intraday spike toward ~31–35%.

Gold impact: Near term: Bowman's cautious tone mildly supports an October 'skip' – a bit less USD pressure on gold than the peak CPI reaction. Medium term: she didn't rule out another hike; Thursday's PPI and retail sales, plus Chair Warsh, are the next tests.
