September PPI runs double the forecast (+1.3% m/m, 6.3% y/y), retail sales +0.8% – gold dips for five minutes, then pops to 4,167

InflationNeutralOct 15, 8:30 AM ET (BLS, Census); gold marks through 9:34 AM ET

BLS (8:30 AM ET): September PPI rose 1.3% m/m (est. 0.6%; prior revised to 0.5%) and 6.3% y/y, the biggest monthly and annual gains since 2022. Energy +10.8%, gasoline +20.3%; PPI ex food and energy +0.7% (est. 0.3%), ex food, energy and trade just +0.5%. Census: retail sales +0.8% (est. 0.3%), ex-autos +0.9% (est. 0.6%), but ex autos and gas only +0.25% after about 1.1% in August. CME FedWatch (via CNBC): October hike odds near 34%, up from under 18% a week ago. Spot gold (CNBC XAU=): 4,149.80 at the print, a 4,138.10 low within five minutes, a 4,167.10 high at 9:15 AM ET, and 4,156.20 at 9:30 AM ET.

Gold impact: Near term: hot data 'should' hit gold, but with energy doing most of the PPI damage and non-gas spending slowing, bonds caught a bid and yields fell, so gold bounced. If yields turn back up, that bounce is at risk. Longer term: 6.3% producer inflation tends to filter into consumer prices, which gives the Fed cover to hike again at the Oct. 27–28 meeting and keeps pressure on gold; the Iran war both feeds inflation and keeps haven demand alive.

Hormuz heats up: Singapore-flagged boxship sunk, IRGC 'counting the moments' to hit U.S. warships – reports Iran is evacuating bases

War & geopoliticsBullish gold ↑Oct 14, 8:40 PM ET (evacuation reports, unconfirmed) · 9:35 PM ET (CNBC Daily Open)

CNBC Daily Open (9:35 PM ET Oct 14): the Singapore-registered container ship GFS Pride was hit by an unidentified projectile, caught fire and sank in the Strait of Hormuz; all 21 crew were evacuated safely. Gold impact: Near term: rising U.S.–Iran escalation risk supports haven demand, especially if the evacuation reports are confirmed or a strike actually lands. The oil channel cuts the other way by feeding inflation and Fed hike bets, so gold benefits most when war headlines hit as a surprise. Longer term: a drawn-out conflict (the IMF now assumes Hormuz reopens only in March 2027) keeps a floor under haven and central bank demand.
