Gold macro brief, Nov 3, 2026 – Gold rebounds 1.8% to ~$3,944–$3,946 from a one-year low; silver climbs 2.9%–3.7% back above $50 as oil’s slide does the heavy lifting

Gold rebounds 1.8% to ~$3,944–$3,946 from a one-year low; silver climbs 2.9%–3.7% back above $50 as oil’s slide does the heavy lifting

GoldBullish gold ↑Nov 2, 2:54 PM ET (CNBC spot print) — Kitco PM U.S. close wrap

CNBC: spot gold rose 1.8% to $3,943.62/oz at 2:54 p.m. ET Monday, after Friday’s 3.6% drop that marked the lowest since October 2025. December COMEX futures settled 1.8% higher at $3,969.60. Kitco’s PM wrap had spot near $3,945.80 (+$71.40, +1.84%) and silver near $50.80 (+$1.80, +3.67%). Gold’s 14-day RSI lifted to about 37 from below 30 on Friday — out of technically oversold territory. Zaner Metals’ Peter Grant: softer oil is easing inflation worries, though the market still leans toward a December Fed hike even as those odds have edged down from last week.

Gold impact: Near term: this is a technical bounce after the month-end flush, helped by oil’s 7% slide and a softer dollar/yields backdrop — not a trend flip yet. Kitco maps resistance at $3,978 then $4,035; support at $3,817 then $3,719. Bigger picture: bullion is still roughly 28%–30% below January’s ~$5,600 peak. Julius Baer’s Carsten Menke argues U.S. deficits persist whatever the midterms bring, so the debt/interest burden remains a longer-term gold support — but a ~73%–75% December hike probability is still the main near-term lid.

🔗 Source: CNBC – Gold rises from one-year low on softer oil; U.S. midterms in focus · Kitco PM Report – Gold, silver recover from October rout as oil drops, Fed tightening bets persist

Brent settles −7.6% at $104.77, WTI −7.4% at $96.46 — no Iran deal yet, but Trump says one “could happen”

OilBullish gold ↑Nov 2 U.S. settle (~2:30–3:30 PM ET) — Qatar mediators to Tehran Monday evening ET

Reuters: Brent futures settled $8.66 (−7.63%) at $104.77/bbl; WTI dropped $7.66 (−7.36%) to $96.46 — Kitco called it the largest daily percentage decline for both since July. The move followed Iranian President Pezeshkian saying the Supreme Leader had granted the government “certain authorities” for talks; Trump said Iran wants a deal and that one “could happen”; Qatari mediators flew to Tehran Monday evening to meet Foreign Minister Araqchi. Tehran says its seven-step plan has been condensed and could be implemented in two days (reopening Hormuz if the U.S. lifts its port blockade). Reuters: Doha talks ended without a breakthrough; Iranian sources still see a fresh U.S. strike as more likely than a deal.

Gold impact: Near term: the oil dump eases energy-inflation fears — that helped gold bounce and took some heat out of yields and the dollar. Longer term: there is still no signed deal. Raymond James’ Molchanov flags many “false dawns”; Goldman Sachs still sketches Brent at $140 in 2027 if Gulf output stays ~3.5 mb/d below the 2025 average. Any Hormuz setback can reprice oil and hike odds higher again, and that would cap gold.

🔗 Source: Reuters via Yahoo Finance – Oil drops 7% as Iran signals openness to peace talks · CNBC – Gold rises on softer oil after third day of Iran–Qatar talks

U.S. 10-year yield eases to 4.906% (−2 bps), DXY near 101.73 (−0.14%) as oil plunges into the midterms

YieldsBullish gold ↑Nov 2 U.S. session — yields wrap afternoon ET; DXY into the U.S. close

CNBC: Treasuries were mixed Monday — the 10-year yield fell more than 2 bps to 4.906%; the 30-year eased 3 bps to 5.279%; the 2-year traded up more than 1 bp at 4.594%. The dollar index slipped 0.14% to 101.73; EUR/USD rose 0.33% to $1.1195. Secretary of State Rubio said Trump wants to resolve the Iran war “diplomatically.” Oil and yields have often moved together this year, though that link loosened in October when AI/Big Tech took the spotlight. Equities: Dow +0.4% (~51,984), S&P 500 −0.5% (7,541), Nasdaq −1.1% — tech stayed soft even as cheaper oil helped other groups.

Gold impact: Near term: softer yields and a softer dollar are the right backdrop for a gold bounce, especially after last week’s liquidity flush. Longer term: December hike odds still sit near 73%, so real-yield relief is capped. Any hot jobs or inflation print this week can shove yields and the dollar back up and cut the rebound short.

🔗 Source: CNBC – Treasury yields mixed as oil prices tumble ahead of the midterms · CNBC – Dollar declines ahead of U.S. midterms as oil falls

FedWatch (Investing.com/CME) as of Nov 2 close: 73.4% chance of a 25 bp December hike / 26.6% hold — January still leans 4.00%–4.25%

For Jan 27, 2027: hold 19.0%; 4.00%–4.25% 60.0%; 4.25%–4.50% 21.0%. CNBC the same day cited CME near ~75% for a December hike, down from ~80% a week earlier as oil softened. Gold impact: Near term: December hike odds still dominate, so gold will struggle to hold above $4,000 without a softer oil or jobs follow-through. Longer term: a real Hormuz reopening plus soft NFP/CPI could lift the hold probability quickly; hot prints do the opposite and keep a lid on bullion.
