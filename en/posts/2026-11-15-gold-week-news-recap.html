📰 Gold newsWeek in the news · Nov 15, 2026· Ngọc Huy

Gold sheds more than 4% on the week as hot CPI pushes yields above 5%

Gold gave back the whole post-midterm mood in the week of Nov. 9–13. It was back above $4,100 on Monday and below $4,000 by midweek. CNBC said spot gold shed more than 4% on the week, its worst week since early June, while Kitco put the loss at 5.00% with spot at $3,883.98 an ounce late Friday. The weekly high was $4,148.95 on Monday and the low was $3,852.10 on Thursday morning, the weakest level since October 2025, according to Kitco.

Monday's bounce was an oil trade. President Trump said fresh strikes on Iran were highly unlikely while talks progressed, Brent fell 5.4% to $92.97 a barrel in Monday trading, the dollar softened, and spot gold rose 1.6% to $4,129.28 (CNBC). It did not survive the inflation data.

October CPI rose 0.4% on Tuesday against a 0.3% forecast, with core up 0.3% versus 0.2% expected, per CNBC citing the BLS. Hours earlier Fed Governor Christopher Waller had said a half-point December hike should be on the table if core came in around 0.3%. CNBC cited CME FedWatch after the release at 88% for a quarter-point hike and 11% for a half-point move, up from zero a week earlier. The 10-year yield jumped nearly 10 basis points to 4.95%. On Wednesday gold broke decisively below $4,000 as Fed officials kept up the hawkish tone (Kitco).

Thursday did the real damage. Trump posted that the U.S. did not need a deal with Iran, Brent rose more than 3% to $95.82, and the 10-year yield surged more than 13 basis points to 5.125% (CNBC). With the dollar index near a 19-month high, spot gold fell 2.7% to $3,881.70 by 1:36 p.m. ET. The lesson of the week: Middle East risk is no longer a clean bid for gold, because higher oil means hotter inflation and a Fed that has to keep tightening.

Friday's October PPI rose 0.3%, below the 0.4% forecast, but the annual rate climbed to 6.5%, the highest since November 2022, and core PPI excluding food, energy and trade services rose 0.5% (CNBC). Bargain hunters lifted spot to $3,922.97 on the release before it faded; CNBC later had spot up 0.6% at $3,898.84 and December futures at $3,903.00, while Kitco showed spot near $3,883 in late U.S. trading. After PPI, CNBC cited FedWatch at 89.4% for a quarter-point December hike and 10.6% for a half point, down from 13.8% before the data. The 10-year still finished the day at 5.143%, about 30 basis points higher on the week per CME Group. Brent settled at $95.84 and WTI at $88.56, both down for a second straight week (Kitco).

The chain driving gold is simple right now: oil, inflation, the Fed, then yields and the dollar. With the 10-year above 5% and markets treating a Dec. 9 hike as a lock (CNBC cited 100% FedWatch odds of a hike), rallies have little room to run. Sentiment has turned with price: 62% of Wall Street analysts in Kitco's survey expect lower gold next week, while retail voters split 42% higher and 38% lower. The levels that matter are $4,000 overhead and the $3,852 weekly low. Next week brings Fed Chair Kevin Warsh on Monday, October retail sales on Tuesday and the minutes of the October FOMC meeting on Wednesday afternoon (Kitco). A durable bounce likely needs softer yields and a weaker dollar, or a Fed that talks down a half-point hike.
