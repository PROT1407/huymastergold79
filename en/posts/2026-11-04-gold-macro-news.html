Gold slips ~0.5%–0.55% to around $3,924–$3,927; silver −2.1% back under $50 as hot JOLTS lifts yields and the dollar — softer oil isn’t enough

GoldBearish gold ↓Nov 3 Kitco PM U.S. close wrap — CNBC spot print during the U.S. session

Kitco’s PM wrap: spot gold near $3,924.10 (−$21.60, −0.55%) and silver near $49.450 (−$1.076, −2.13%) in late U.S. trade Tuesday. CNBC: spot at $3,927.49 (−0.5%) after a session low of $3,893.31; December COMEX futures −0.8% at $3,936.80. The seller was data — stronger September JOLTS and factory orders (+1.8%) pushed the 10-year yield up about 6 bps to ~4.951% and firmed the dollar. Another day of lower oil helped only briefly. Markets are waiting on midterm results plus Wednesday’s ADP/ISM services prints and Friday’s payrolls.

Gold impact: Near term: Monday’s oil-led bounce got clipped; bullion is back testing the $3,930–$3,900 area. Kitco maps resistance at $3,975 then $4,084; support at $3,871 then $3,797. Bigger picture: December hike odds still dominate, so real yields remain the lid. Softer ADP/NFP or a clear Hormuz deal would reopen a sustained push above $4,000 — until then, rallies stay sellable.

Brent settles −1.1% at $103.60, WTI −0.4% at $96.09 — Qatar still mediating; Iran rejects “coercion” diplomacy; U.S. hit Qeshm Island

OilBullish gold ↑Nov 3 U.S. settle — Qatar briefing / Iran response during the U.S. day

CNBC/Kitco: Brent futures settled $1.17 (−1.1%) at $103.60/bbl; WTI lost $0.37 (−0.4%) to $96.09 — a second down day after Monday’s 7% slide. The driver: deal chatter. Qatar’s Foreign Ministry said a delegation met Tehran and both sides are reviewing mediator proposals. Iran’s FM spokesman Baqaei (IRNA): Tehran will not accept diplomacy based on coercion. WSJ via CNBC: the U.S. struck Iran’s Qeshm Island in the Strait of Hormuz on Monday — officials called it a limited, defensive action. UKMTO: at least four ship attacks since the start of the month. Aramco’s CEO: Hormuz flows remain below pre-conflict levels.

Gold impact: Near term: softer oil eases energy-inflation pressure — that would help gold if yields weren’t rising. Longer term: there is still no deal; Iranian officials say one remains distant; the Qeshm strike and ship attacks keep supply risk alive. Any Hormuz setback can reprice Brent and December hike odds higher again, and that would cap bullion.

But hot JOLTS/NFP keeps December hike odds elevated, so any gold bounce still has to clear the real-yield bar.
