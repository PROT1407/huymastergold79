Gold closes the week below $4,000 for the first time since mid-July, down about 4% – worst week since June as the AI selloff triggers margin calls

Cross-assetBearish gold ↓Oct 23, 2:06 PM ET (Dow Jones) · 2:27 PM ET (CNBC quote)

CNBC (Reuters): spot gold fell 0.6% to $3,998.49/oz at 2:27 PM ET Friday, slipping below $4,000 for the first time since mid-July and losing about 4% on the week. December futures settled down 1.3% at $4,007.90. Dow Jones Market Data: front-month Comex gold settled at $3,991.10, down $168.40 (−4.05%) for the week – the lowest settle since July 16 and 24.96% below the Jan. 29, 2026 record of $5,318.40. Jim Wyckoff of American Gold Exchange said inflation worries mean central banks can't ease as fast as they'd like, and the tech selloff has forced margin-call selling in gold and silver. Spot silver slid 1% to $53.97, its lowest since November 2025.

Gold impact: Near term: a weekly close under $4,000 is a bad technical look – $4,000 flips to resistance for Monday, and bulls need it back fast or the selling keeps coming. Longer term: as long as crude holds above $110 and the market keeps pricing a December hike, the $4,100–$4,150 base is out of reach; Asian physical demand (India picked up around Dussehra) is a slow-moving floor, not a catalyst.

Brent settles at $116.72 (+3.1%), highest since April 29 and up 6.2% on the week as Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia continue and the US pulls nonessential staff

OilBearish gold ↓Oct 23, 2:30 PM ET (oil settlement)

CNBC: Brent rose 3.1% to $116.72 a barrel, its highest settle since April 29; WTI added 3.8% to $103.27. Brent gained 6.2% on the week, its third straight weekly gain. Traders increasingly expect the US to resume strikes on Iran in the coming weeks, and Iran's army chief said Tehran is ready for "asymmetric warfare" (Tasnim). The State Department ordered all nonessential personnel out of Saudi Arabia, and Aramco told staff to evacuate its Dhahran headquarters. Reuters: the UAE reported an oil spill from an unidentified tanker off Umm Al Quwain.

Gold impact: Near term: every leg higher in crude pushes inflation expectations and yields up – that, not the haven bid, is what sank gold this week. Longer term: a real Gulf supply disruption could eventually feed the inflation-hedge case for gold, but only once the Fed stops threatening hikes.

US flash PMI: selling prices rise fastest since Aug. 2022 (59.8); Michigan 1-year inflation expectations 4.7%, long-run 3.5% – both highest since May

InflationBearish gold ↓Oct 23, 9:45 AM ET (PMI) · 10:00 AM ET (Michigan)

Reuters (via Investing.com): S&P Global's flash October composite PMI eased to 57.6 from a five-year high of 58.4 (in line with the 57.6 Econoday consensus); manufacturing 56.0 vs. 55.5 expected, services 57.9 vs. 58.0. The output-prices gauge jumped to 59.8, the highest since August 2022, and new orders hit 58.2, the highest since March 2022. University of Michigan (final October): sentiment 48.0 vs. 48.1 in September; 1-year inflation expectations 4.7% (from 4.6%) and long-run 3.5% (from 3.4%), both the highest since May; 69% of consumers expect rates to rise over the next year, the most since April 2023.

Gold impact: Near term: hotter selling prices plus rising inflation expectations strengthen the case for a December hike – a headwind for gold. Longer term: with S&P Global's survey pointing to ~5% annualized Q4 growth, the Fed has no reason to pivot soon; gold only wins this one if inflation gets ahead of the Fed and real yields fall.

🔗 Source: Reuters (via Investing.com) – US business activity cools in October; strong demand fueling price pressures · University of Michigan – Surveys of Consumers, final October 2026

Odds of an Oct. 28 hike tick up to 10.3%; December still ~75% – Fed in blackout, Oct. 29 PCE is the next test

FedBearish gold ↓Oct 23, 8:25 PM ET (Fed Rate Monitor)

Investing.com Fed Rate Monitor (based on CME 30-day fed funds futures, updated 8:25 PM ET Friday): Oct. 27–28 meeting – hold at 3.75%–4.00% 89.7%, 25 bp hike 10.3% (prior day 92.0% / 8.0%). Dec. 8–9 meeting – hold 24.9%, +25 bps 67.6%, +50 bps 7.5% (≈ 75% total odds of a hike). CNBC, citing CME FedWatch: 92% odds of no change next week and 78% odds of a December hike. The Fed is in its pre-meeting blackout, so no Fed speakers; the PCE report, the Fed's preferred inflation gauge, is due Oct. 29.

Gold impact: Near term: a hold next week is all but priced, so the risk is the press-conference tone – a hawkish lean adds pressure on gold. Longer term: as long as December hike odds sit near 75%, gold lacks a durable bid; it takes a softer PCE and a pullback in those odds to give bulls a real opening.

US envoy Tom Barrack: an Iran strike "absolutely" could come this weekend – Trump says he "doesn't know anything about it"; Israel votes Oct. 27

War & geopoliticsNeutralOct 23, 6:43 PM ET (AFP report)

AFP (via The Hindu): Tom Barrack, US ambassador to Turkey, told podcast host Mario Nawfal the US could strike Iran as soon as this weekend, though he has no direct role on Iran; he also said Washington isn't seeking regime change. Asked in Florida, President Trump said, "If that's the case, I don't know anything about it." Trump had earlier said strikes would not come before the Nov. 3 midterms. Israeli Prime Minister Netanyahu, facing an election Tuesday, Oct. 27, has repeatedly vowed to topple Iran's government.

Gold impact: Near term: with markets shut, any weekend military action hits the Sunday-evening open (ET) – expect a gap either way: an initial haven bid, but if crude spikes, December hike bets can drag gold right back down like this week. Longer term: a drawn-out conflict keeps a geopolitical premium in gold, but the oil-inflation-rates channel is currently doing more of the work.

🔗 Source: AFP (via The Hindu) – U.S. could strike Iran this weekend, envoy says
