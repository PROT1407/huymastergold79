H79 Gold Weekly Recap, Oct. 26–30: Gold Drops 3% to a One-Year Low – The Downtrend Holds as November Opens the Leg Toward $3,600–$3,500

The week in brief: Gold opened at 3,996.39, spiked to 4,078.59 right after Wednesday's Fed decision, got sold back down to 3,870.41 and closed at 3,875.06. That's $119.59 lower on the week (−3.0%), with Friday alone down 3.6% – the lowest close in more than a year. October finished down 6.8% (4,156.24 → 3,875.06), a second straight monthly loss. Last week's bearish call played out, with price driving through 3,900.

Technicals: the downtrend is well defined:
Since the Aug. 25 peak at 4,694.84, every rally has stalled below the last one: 4,509.89 (Sept. 3), 4,223.95 (Oct. 2), 4,153.48 (Oct. 20) and 4,078.59 after the Fed. Lows keep stepping down too: 4,068.8 in early October, 3,978.1 last week, 3,870.41 now.
The 4,100–4,150 base that held through September and October is broken, and it lines up with the 50% retracement of the entire 2,614 → 5,585 rally (January 2025 – January 2026) at 4,100. This week also took out June's 3,943 low, the level that held all summer.
Overhead, the 3,902–3,964 4H fair value gap left by Friday's flush and the 4,079–4,106 daily gap now act as supply. Any talk of a reversal starts with reclaiming 4,100, and there's no sign of that.
Below, the next marker is the October 2025 low near 3,820, then the 61.8% retracement at 3,749. Beyond that sits 3,600–3,500, just above the April–August 2025 range (tops at 3,435–3,500) that launched the September 2025 breakout.

Macro: the Fed isn't done: The Fed held, but two members wanted a hike now; GDP grew 3.4% and PCE inflation hit 3.6% – the economy is running hot and inflation is sticky. FedWatch puts the odds of a December hike near 75%. With the 10-year at 4.93% against 3.6% inflation, real yields are clearly positive, which makes holding a non-yielding asset like gold more expensive. The dollar is holding around 102. Hormuz-driven crude strength adds to the inflation risk rather than pulling haven money into gold. And when tech sells off, gold gets liquidated alongside it – right now it's a source of cash, not a haven.

November opens the year-end leg: Our 3,600–3,500 target, set when gold broke the 4,100–4,150 base, stands, and that move is under way. November opens the final stretch of Q4: a December Fed meeting with a live hike, plus year-end portfolio rebalancing – a backdrop that can extend the decline while rates are still pointed higher. Bounces into 3,902–3,964 would be technical rallies inside a downtrend. The trend only turns if gold reclaims 4,100 and the Fed changes its tune.

What to watch, Nov. 2–6 (ET):
Mon. Nov. 2, 10:00 AM: ISM manufacturing.
Tue. Nov. 3: U.S. midterm elections – results come in overnight, so expect sharp swings.
Wed. Nov. 4, 10:00 AM: ISM services.
Fri. Nov. 6, 8:30 AM: October payrolls (consensus +63K, prior +29K), unemployment rate (4.2% expected) and average hourly earnings (+0.3% expected). A strong jobs number adds to the case for a December hike.

Gold opened the week of Oct. 26 at 3,996.39, tagged its high of 4,078.59 right after Wednesday's Fed decision, then slid to 3,870.41 and closed at 3,875.06 – down $119.59 (−3.0%) and its lowest close in more than a year. Friday alone took off 3.6%, the biggest one-day drop since June. The Fed held at 3.75%–4.00%, but two dissenters wanted a hike now and Chair Warsh offered nothing on December. Third-quarter GDP came in at 3.4% the next morning and September PCE inflation ran 3.6%. On Friday night St. Louis Fed President Musalem said more tightening is needed "soon." Another tanker was hit near the Strait of Hormuz, and crude added more than 2% Friday. The tell came Friday: the 10-year yield eased to 4.93% and the dollar index sat flat at 101.96, yet gold still fell $145. Tech led a broad equity selloff (S&P 500 −1.8%), and the liquidation dragged gold and silver (−6.7%) down with it. When gold can't hold up with yields and the dollar both lower, buyers are genuinely weak.
