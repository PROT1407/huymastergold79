Gold opened the week of Oct. 26 at 3,996.39, tagged its high of 4,078.59 right after Wednesday's Fed decision, then slid to 3,870.41 and closed at 3,875.06 – down $119.59 (−3.0%) and its lowest close in more than a year. Friday alone took off 3.6%, the biggest one-day drop since June. The whole week told one story: the Fed isn't done. The FOMC held at 3.75%–4.00%, but two members dissented in favor of a hike and Chair Warsh offered no guidance on December. A day later Q3 GDP came in at 3.4% and September PCE ran 3.6% – a hot economy with inflation that won't cool. Friday, St. Louis Fed President Musalem said more tightening is needed "and soon," and FedWatch still prices roughly 75% odds of a December hike. Crude piled on: another tanker attack in the Strait of Hormuz lifted oil more than 2% on Friday. Here's the tell: on Friday the 10-year slipped to 4.93% and the dollar index sat flat at 101.96, and gold still dropped $145. A tech-led stock rout (S&P 500 −1.8%) triggered liquidation across metals, with silver down 6.7%. When gold can't hold up on a softer dollar and lower yields, the bulls are in real trouble. My take: the downtrend is intact, and the 3,900–3,965 gap left by Friday's flush is now supply. Next week I'm selling bounces and I'm not bottom-fishing until we reach the 3,800–3,815 support.
