Gold ends Monday up 0.6% at $4,020.60 as crude cools and dip buyers step in – traders park near $4,000 into the Fed

FedBullish gold ↑Oct 26, 2:09 PM ET (CNBC)

CNBC (Reuters): spot gold rose 0.6% to $4,020.60 an ounce by 2:09 PM ET, bouncing off Friday's $3,975 low – the weakest since July 20. December futures settled 0.8% higher at $4,038.40. StoneX's Bob Haberkorn credited the pullback in crude plus some bargain hunting, but expects light trade and prices hanging "around the $4,000 level" into the Fed announcement. CME FedWatch puts December hike odds near 80%; PCE is due Thursday.

Gold impact: Near term: the bounce has real drivers (softer oil, steady long-end yields), but liquidity is thin ahead of the FOMC. $4,000 is the pivot; bulls need a clean break above 4,040 to show they're back. Longer term: December hike odds of roughly 70%–80% keep real rates high, and that's still the main headwind until the Fed sounds less hawkish.

🔗 Source: CNBC – Gold rises as lower oil prices ease inflation fears ahead of Fed meeting

Crude settles lower – Brent $115.81, WTI $101.76 – as Saudi defense chief meets Iran's president, but Houthi strikes continue and the SPR sits at a 1982 low

OilBullish gold ↑Oct 26 settlement (CNBC)

CNBC: Brent lost 91 cents to settle at $115.81 after briefly topping $117; WTI fell $1.51 to $101.76. Crude is still up about 12% this month. Saudi Defense Minister Prince Khalid bin Salman met Iranian President Masoud Pezeshkian in Tehran, with Pakistani and Turkish officials attending. Per Reuters, the Houthis claimed hits on pumping stations along Saudi Arabia's East-West pipeline, and Egypt's parliament authorized President el-Sisi to take "all necessary measures" to help defend the kingdom. The Strategic Petroleum Reserve fell to 282.6 million barrels, the lowest since October 1982.

Gold impact: Near term: Saudi-Iran talks take some heat out of crude, easing the inflation and yield pressure that's been capping gold. Longer term: the war premium is still baked into oil and a depleted SPR limits Washington's ability to cap prices – one successful strike on Saudi infrastructure sends crude and yields higher and puts gold back on the defensive.

🔗 Source: CNBC – Oil ticks lower as Saudi defense minister meets with Iran's president in Tehran to de-escalate · Reuters (via Korea Times) – Oil stays near six-month highs on Saudi attacks

Fed set to pause, "but maybe not for long" – inflation is broadening and December hike odds climb to 72.1%

FOMCBearish gold ↓Oct 26, 7:05 PM ET (Investing.com)

Reuters: Fed officials have telegraphed no move this week (3.75%–4.00%), but September inflation broadened well beyond fuel, Q3 growth accelerated and unemployment is steady at 4.2%. Nationwide's Kathy Bostjancic says the data support a hike, and that the Fed only blinked because the 10-year spiked toward quarter-century highs after the September move – New York Fed President John Williams and Vice Chair Philip Jefferson both signaled patience. Investing.com's Fed Rate Monitor (CME-based) as of 7:05 PM ET: 89.7% odds of a hold on Wednesday; December hike odds up to 72.1% from 67.6%. The decision is at 2:00 PM ET Wednesday.

Gold impact: Near term: the hold is priced, so the move comes from the statement and Warsh's presser – if he keeps December live, the dollar and 2-year yields firm and gold struggles to hold $4,000. Longer term: the Fed is still tightening into broadening inflation, and high real rates keep weighing on gold until inflation rolls over.

🔗 Source: Reuters (via U.S. News) – Fed to hit pause on rate hikes, but maybe not for long · Investing.com Fed Rate Monitor (CME fed funds futures), Oct 26, 7:05 PM ET

10-year yield flat at 4.987% while the 2-year climbs to 4.712% after a soft $69 billion auction; Deutsche Bank still sees a hawkish Fed message

YieldsNeutralOct 26, 3:46 PM ET (WSJ)

WSJ: as of 3:46 PM ET, the 10-year yield was unchanged at 4.987%, the 2-year rose 3.2 basis points to 4.712%, and the 30-year slipped 1.4 bps to 5.300%. The $69 billion 2-year auction tailed, a sign of soft demand (BMO). Deutsche Bank, via CNBC, says the message will stay hawkish even with a pause this week, and expects Chair Warsh to stress that tighter financial conditions are no substitute for higher policy rates. Up next: September durable goods (WSJ consensus +1.7%) and Conference Board consumer confidence.

Gold impact: Near term: a firmer 2-year says the market is still holding its hike bets, which caps gold's bounce; a flat long end means no fresh pressure for now. Longer term: a 10-year near 5% is a steep opportunity cost for a non-yielding asset – the trend in gold won't turn until long yields come down for real.

🔗 Source: WSJ – Treasury yields mixed as markets price in a Fed hold · CNBC – Treasury yields little changed as investors look ahead to the Fed's rate decision

Cross-assetNeutralOct 26, 4:00 PM ET (CNBC)
