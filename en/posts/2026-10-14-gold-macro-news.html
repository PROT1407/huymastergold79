Houthi missile hits Saudi Aramco HQ building in Dhahran after Saudi strikes on Sanaa – Brent edges up to ~$106.40 in Asia

War & geopoliticsNeutralTuesday evening Oct 13, Saudi time (UTC+3) ≈ early afternoon ET; Reuters on the Sanaa strikes Oct 13, 12:25 PM ET; Brent/gold quoted Oct 13, 7:45 PM ET

Reuters: Saudi Arabia's Energy Ministry confirmed an Aramco administrative building in Dhahran was hit Tuesday evening (Saudi time), causing a limited fire and no casualties. A company source said a ballistic missile struck the office tower, damaging the top three floors. Earlier, the Saudi-led coalition hit the Sabah fuel depot outside Sanaa, Yemen; the Houthis threatened both Aramco's headquarters and a Riyadh refinery. Debris from an intercepted missile injured four people in Riyadh. President Trump spoke with Crown Prince Mohammed bin Salman; the US has not joined the fighting directly. Iran has kept the Strait of Hormuz closed since February, which makes the Bab el-Mandeb route even more important. Markets: Brent (BZ=F, Yahoo) $106.39 at 7:45 PM ET Oct 13 vs. a $105.69 settle; COMEX gold (GC=F) flat around $4,179–4,184 (6:00–7:45 PM ET). No CNBC story on the Aramco attack yet.

Gold impact: Near term: geopolitical risk is a safe-haven tailwind for gold, but the bigger effect runs through oil – higher crude → hotter inflation → more reason for the Fed to hike → firmer dollar and yields → headwind for gold. The two forces offset, so gold barely moved overnight. Bigger picture: if the Houthis keep hitting Saudi oil infrastructure, crude can spike and pull rate-hike bets higher; if the US or Turkey gets drawn in and the war escalates, the safe-haven bid for gold should become clearer.

Kazakhstan flips from buyer to seller: cutting gold to 75% of reserves from 78% by end-2026

Central banksBearish gold ↓Bloomberg reported Oct 12; Kitco posted Oct 13, 11:37 AM ET

Bloomberg (via Oninvest) and Kitco (posted 11:37 AM ET Oct 13): Kazakhstan central bank Deputy Governor Aliya Moldabekova said 24-year-high Treasury yields make the dollar more attractive and gold less so; gold already makes up about 78% of FX reserves (386 tonnes, $66.4B in reserves at end-September) – 'like a single-asset portfolio'. The target is 75% by year-end, half via selling options and half via physical sales (local press estimates roughly 12 tonnes, ~$1.6B). If gold drops below $4,000, the share could fall to target on its own.

Gold impact: Near term: bad for sentiment, since central-bank buying has been a pillar of the multi-year rally; ~12 tonnes isn't large, but it adds supply around current prices. Bigger picture: if other central banks rebalance while US yields are high, official-sector support weakens; on the other hand China has bought 23 months in a row, so it's not a broad trend yet. $4,000 is the level to watch.

🔗 Source: Kitco – 'Single-asset portfolio': Kazakhstan plans gold sales as holdings reach 78% of reserves · Oninvest (via Bloomberg) – Kazakhstan switching from being one of the world's biggest gold buyers to a seller
