Warsh at the IMF in Bangkok: 'I don't prejudge meetings' – no October hike signal, dollar slides to a near two-week low

FedBullish gold ↑Oct 15, 11:30 PM ET (Warsh at the IMF, Bangkok); price reaction per FXStreet at 1:35 AM ET Oct 16

CNBC: in a fireside chat with IMF Managing Director Kristalina Georgieva, Fed Chair Kevin Warsh declined to say whether the Fed will hike again this month: 'I don't prejudge meetings. I don't believe in saying now what I might do at the meetings that we have in the balance of this year,' reiterating his opposition to forward guidance. He described a 'synchronous tightening' by central banks worldwide, meaning 'there's no free riders,' and said the Fed will deliver price stability. These were his last public remarks before the blackout starts Saturday ahead of the Oct 27–28 FOMC. FXStreet: the dollar came under fresh selling and gold drew dip buyers from the $4,150 area.

Gold impact: Near term: no new hawkish signal, so the street is close to pricing out an October hike; a weaker dollar supports gold. But Warsh didn't rule a hike out either, so the data between now and Oct 28 calls the shots, and gold will swing hard on every print. Longer term: a Fed without forward guidance means bigger moves around each meeting, and synchronized global tightening remains a headwind for bullion.
