📰 Gold newsWeek in the news · Nov 8, 2026· Ngọc Huy

Gold rose 4.8% last week: midterms lifted prices, Friday's jobs report cooled the rally

Last week (Nov. 2 through the Nov. 6 U.S. session), gold recovered sharply before cooling into the weekend. According to CNBC, spot gold gained 4.8% on the week. As of CNBC's 1:06 p.m. ET update on Nov. 6, spot was down 0.7% at $4,059.90 an ounce after a session low of $4,033.29. December U.S. gold futures were down 1% at $4,071.80 at the same update. On Oct. 30, gold had fallen to a one-year low of $3,869.85.

The midweek impulse came from the U.S. midterm elections. Reuters (via Kitco) reported that Democrats won both the House and the Senate; the dollar and Treasury yields fell, which supported gold. On Nov. 4, spot climbed nearly 3.4% — the strongest daily gain since Aug. 19 — to $4,046.74, with a session high of $4,050.99. On Nov. 5 the advance continued: spot rose 1.4% to $4,117.69 at 1:16 p.m. GMT and hit a session high of $4,146.63, a level last seen on Oct. 20. By Thursday, Reuters said gold was up more than $250 from that one-year low. Hopes that post-election tensions between the U.S. and Iran might ease pulled Brent down nearly 9% on Wednesday and left it below $100 a barrel on Thursday, taking some inflation pressure off bullion.

Friday, Nov. 6, brought a shift after the October employment report. CNBC, citing BLS data, said payrolls rose by 165,000 (versus a Dow Jones forecast of 59,000), the unemployment rate fell to 4.1%, hourly earnings rose more than expected, and the August and September figures were revised higher. The dollar rebounded 0.7% to its highest level since Oct. 29, and 10-year Treasury yields also rose, helping pull spot gold down 0.7% on the session.

Rate-hike odds moved with each CME FedWatch print cited in the press: about 82% for a December hike on Nov. 4 and 81% on Nov. 5 (Reuters/Kitco); on the morning of Nov. 6 after the jobs report, CNBC cited 76.8% for the Dec. 8–9 FOMC meeting; later that afternoon CNBC's gold story cited 74.8%, versus 72.8% just before the report. Two Fed officials on Thursday backed small, steady rate increases. Lower prices also encouraged Indian retail buying ahead of Diwali, CNBC said. Markets will keep watching incoming data for clues about the Fed's next step in December.
