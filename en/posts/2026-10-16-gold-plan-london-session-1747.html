Gold Tags 4,191 and Stalls With the 10-Year at 5.1% – Buy the Dip, Don't Chase

Overnight: Thursday's September PPI hit at 8:30 AM ET and ran far hotter than expected: +1.3% m/m (est. 0.6%), core +0.7%, and +6.3% y/y – the biggest gain since 2022 – with retail sales up 0.8% (est. 0.3%). Gold got knocked down to 4,138.10 at 8:35 AM ET as the DXY spiked to 102.26 (8:40 AM ET). The flush didn't last: the 10-year yield rolled over to 5.037% by 10:00 AM ET, AI names took a beating (Nvidia −1.84%, Dell −6.8%), and between 3:00 and 4:00 PM ET gold ripped from 4,157 to 4,176.90. It finished the New York day at 4,170.10 (5:55 PM ET). Anyone who chased the PPI short under 4,140 got squeezed.

Asian session: Gold opened the overnight session at 4,170.20 at 6:00 PM ET and drifted to the Asian low of 4,147.80 at 9:35 PM ET as the DXY firmed to 102.09 and the 10-year climbed to 5.096%. Buyers came back at 10:20 PM ET: in 35 minutes gold ran from 4,153 to 4,178.30 (10:55 PM ET). Fed Chair Kevin Warsh took the stage at 11:30 PM ET; gold wobbled to 4,163.50 (11:40 PM ET) and bounced right back once it was clear he had nothing new – "I don't prejudge meetings." The Asian high of 4,184.10 came at 12:05 AM ET, right as the DXY bottomed at 101.82. Asia closed at 4,176.50 (2:55 AM ET): high 4,184.10, low 4,147.80, a ~$36 range.

London morning: London opened at 4,176.70 at 3:00 AM ET. Gold tagged 4,188.80 at 3:30 AM ET and then a day high of 4,191.30 at 5:15 AM ET. That's where it ran out of gas: the 10-year jumped from 5.073% to 5.102% (4:15 AM ET) and stayed there, and gold slipped to 4,175.20 at 6:05 AM ET – right on the 38.2% retracement of the 4,147.80 → 4,191.30 leg – before bouncing to 4,184. At 6:50 AM ET spot sits near 4,178 (CNBC 4,177.90; gold-api.com 4,178.90), up about $8 from the New York close. The tell: the DXY slid further to 101.58 (6:40 AM ET) and gold didn't follow – yields are in the driver's seat. Neither of this morning's buy setups (4,150–4,160 and 4,160–4,167) filled, and the 4,203–4,212 short never triggered; the dip stopped at 4,175.20. I'd rather miss a move than chase one.

Why gold rose – and why it stalled: First, a softer dollar: the DXY fell from 102.26 overnight to 101.61 (−0.41% on the day) and is headed for its first weekly loss in five; sterling hit a three-week high on solid UK data (CNBC). That's what lifted gold to 4,191. Second, the Fed: Warsh kept his cards close, so markets still price ~87% odds of an October hold but ~91% odds of a December hike (CME FedWatch via CNBC) – higher-for-longer remains the long-run headwind. Third, yields: the 10-year at 5.102% and the 2-year at 4.751% are still elevated after 3.7% CPI and 6.3% PPI; that's the brake that kept gold under 4,191 even as the dollar fell. Fourth, oil and the war: Brent is near $105.90 with Strait of Hormuz traffic only ~68% of normal (Reuters); President Trump says Iran asked for a halt to strikes and talks are under way, but he hasn't ruled out more (Middle East Eye) – expensive oil keeps inflation fears alive. Fifth, sentiment: the 20 biggest AI and chip stocks have shed more than $1 trillion this week and CoreWeave's five-year CDS briefly topped 1,140 bps (CNBC), yet European stocks are up today (STOXX 600 +0.7%) and the VIX is 16.1 – no panic bid to force a breakout. Longer term, ANZ says a retreat in yields and the dollar would pave the way for gold above $5,000 in 2027 (CNBC).

Today's main event: There's no high-impact U.S. data on Friday's ForexFactory calendar: 8:30 AM ET import prices (est. +0.9%, prior 0.7%), 9:15 AM ET industrial production (+0.4%, prior 0.0%) and capacity utilization (76.5%) – all low impact – plus the federal budget balance at 2:00 PM ET. The Fed enters its blackout period Saturday ahead of the Oct. 27–28 meeting, so today's tape will trade off yields, the dollar, Iran/Hormuz headlines and Friday profit-taking. Level to watch for the weekly close: last Friday's ~4,193.80 – settle above it and the week is green; below it, a small weekly loss (CNBC had gold −0.2% on the week at 4,184).
