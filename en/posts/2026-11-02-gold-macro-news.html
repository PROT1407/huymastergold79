Gold macro brief, Nov 2, 2026 – Gold drops 3.7% on the week to ~$3,875 as the tech rout forces liquidation – a modest bounce back to $3,900 at the Sunday open

Gold drops 3.7% on the week to ~$3,875 as the tech rout forces liquidation – a modest bounce back to $3,900 at the Sunday open

Cross-assetBearish gold ↓Oct 30, 4:00 PM ET close → Nov 1, 6:00 PM ET Sunday open

Kitco: spot gold lost 3.5%–3.6% on Friday and finished the week near $3,875 (−3.74% w/w), with a weekly low of $3,870.25 – Kitco's weakest print in more than a year. The weekly high of $4,082.50 came right before the Fed decision. Silver shed about 6% to roughly $49, below $50 for the first time in 11 months. The trigger was a 2.86% slide in the Nasdaq on doubts about AI spending returns, which spilled into forced selling and margin calls across precious metals (Commerzbank, independent trader Tai Wong). Reuters: bullion is headed for a second straight monthly loss. In early Asian trade (fx678), spot traded near $3,892 before reclaiming $3,900, up roughly 0.5%–0.7%.

Gold impact: Near term: the Sunday-night pop is a technical bounce off a month-end flush, not a trend change. Kitco maps resistance at $3,890–$3,910, then $3,943 and $3,975; support at $3,800, then $3,780 – as long as price stays under $3,910, sellers are in control. The tell is that gold fell even as yields and the dollar eased: when equities get hit this hard, gold gets sold to raise cash. Bigger picture: bullion is down about 30% from January's $5,595 record; sub-$4,000 prices tend to draw central-bank buying, but with the Fed still leaning toward a hike, rallies are hard to sustain.

Iran and Qatar hold a third day of talks on a two-day plan to reopen Hormuz – Brent slips more than 1% to $111.92 at the open

War & geopoliticsNeutralOct 30, 2:30 PM ET (oil settle) – Nov 1, 8:30 AM ET (Doha talks) – Nov 1, 6:03 PM ET (Asian open)

CNBC (Reuters): Iranian negotiators met Qatari mediators in Doha for a third straight day on Sunday, pushing for a deal to end the U.S.-Israeli war on Iran. Foreign Minister Abbas Araqchi said talks on a "two-day proposal" – reopening the Strait of Hormuz within two days of an agreement with Washington – are "ongoing and progressing," with no final conclusion. President Trump has said the U.S. won't launch new strikes before Tuesday's midterms but hasn't ruled out action afterward. On Friday a tanker in the strait was hit by limpet mines; December Brent settled at $117.64 (+2.4%), its highest since late April, and WTI at $104.12, with Brent up 13.6% in October. OPEC+ left December output targets unchanged. Reuters at 6:03 PM ET Sunday: Brent −1.33% to $111.92, WTI −1.71% to $102.34.

Gold impact: Near term: crude backing off on diplomacy takes a little heat out of the inflation story and the hike odds, which gives gold some room to bounce. The real risk sits after Nov 3 – if strikes resume, an oil spike lifts hike bets and squeezes gold again, the same pattern we saw all October. Longer term: $100-plus crude keeps inflation sticky and rates higher for longer, which is a headwind for gold; only an actual deal that reopens Hormuz changes that.

Pivotal week: Tuesday's midterms and Friday's 8:30 AM ET payrolls – FedWatch prices about 72% odds of a December hike

FedNeutralNov 2, 10:00 AM ET (ISM) – Nov 3 (midterms) – Nov 6, 8:30 AM ET (payrolls)

CME FedWatch (via FXBus, late Oct 30): 72% odds of a 25-bp hike at the Dec 8–9 meeting vs. 28% for a hold; by January 2027, 58.4% for a cumulative 25 bps and 22.3% for 50 bps. The calendar (FXStreet/XM): ISM manufacturing Monday at 10:00 AM ET; midterm elections Tuesday (Democrats favored to take the House, the Senate a toss-up); ADP and ISM services Wednesday; RBA (Tuesday) and BoE (Thursday) both expected to hike; October nonfarm payrolls Friday at 8:30 AM ET after September's weak +29,000. Earnings: Apple and Palantir Monday, AMD Tuesday, Qualcomm Wednesday. The Oct 28 FOMC held at 3.75%–4.00% with two dissents (Cook, Hammack) for an immediate hike.

Gold impact: Near term: payrolls are the main test – a strong print could lock in December and push gold lower; another soft number like September's opens the door to a relief rally. Midterms: a Republican sweep likely lifts the dollar and yields on tariff and inflation worries, a negative for gold, while a split Congress tends to cool the dollar. And once the vote is done, the risk of renewed U.S. strikes on Iran comes back into play. Longer term: markets price the fed funds rate roughly 70 bps higher by the end of 2027 – nothing yet argues for a turn in gold's bigger trend.

10-year yield ends the week at 4.93%–4.94%, a roughly 7-week low, with the dollar flat near 102 – gold gets no help

YieldsNeutralOct 30, 4:00 PM ET close

Stocktwits/BondStats: the 10-year Treasury yield closed the week at 4.93%–4.94% (−4 bps Friday, −7 bps w/w), the 2-year at 4.56%–4.59% (down about 10 bps on the week), and the 30-year was little changed at 5.31%–5.32%. Money rotated out of AI names (the SOX fell 7.2% Friday) and into duration. The Dollar Index settled at 101.96–101.97, flat on the week (+0.08% Friday).

Gold impact: Near term: falling yields are usually a tailwind for gold, but last week the safety bid went into Treasuries, not bullion – gold was sold to cover equity losses. Once the equity selling calms down, lower yields can help a bounce. Longer term: the 10-year real yield is still around 2.6%, which keeps the opportunity cost of holding gold high until the Fed turns.
