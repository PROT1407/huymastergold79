Q3 GDP runs at 3.4%, September PCE hits 3.6% – gold sheds 1% to ~$4,025 as December hike odds stay above 70%

InflationBearish gold ↓Oct 29, 8:30 AM ET (GDP + PCE) → NY close

BEA/CNBC: third-quarter GDP grew at a 3.4% annualized pace (consensus 3.1%–3.2%, Q2 2.2%). September PCE rose 0.5% m/m and 3.6% y/y (August 3.4%), while core printed 0.3% and 3.1% – right on consensus. Gasoline and energy did the heavy lifting. Initial jobless claims dropped to about 194,000. Kitco had spot gold at $4,024.60 late in New York, down $42.00 (−1.03%), with silver off 3.1% at $52.18, an 11-month low. Most of gold's damage was done in Asia and early London, before the numbers; the print itself got a few dollars of pop that faded right away. CNBC (Reuters): bullion is on track for a second straight monthly loss.

Gold impact: Near term: a hot economy plus 3.6% inflation keeps a December hike on the table, so gold has no fuel for a durable bounce. Kitco maps resistance at 4,084 then 4,146, support at 4,024 then 3,986 – lose 4,024 and the $4,000 handle and 3,986 come into play. Longer term: energy inflation bleeding into services (core services ex-housing at 3.6% y/y) means higher-for-longer rates, so the intermediate trend still leans lower until inflation clearly turns.

ECB holds at 2.5% but flags a December hike – Lagarde says the 'direction of travel' is clear; markets price ~67%

Central banksNeutralOct 29, 9:15 AM ET (decision) – presser 9:45 AM ET

The ECB left the deposit rate at 2.50%, the refi rate at 2.65% and the marginal lending rate at 2.90%, warning that rising energy prices skew inflation risks higher and growth risks lower; euro-area inflation is running at 3.8%, the highest since September 2023 (CNBC). Lagarde said the Governing Council debated a hike before choosing to wait for more data, and that barring a major development 'the direction of travel is quite clear.' Reuters sources say a Dec. 17 hike is all but certain, followed by a pause. Money markets price roughly a 67% chance of a December move (LSEG). Lagarde also announced she will step down early.

Gold impact: Near term: a hawkish ECB lifts the euro and trims the dollar – a modest tailwind for gold. Longer term: major central banks are all leaning toward tightening on the energy shock, and a higher global rate floor remains a shared headwind for bullion.

War & geopoliticsBearish gold ↓Oct 29 → ~2:30 PM ET (crude settlement)

Gold impact: Near term: talk of talks takes some heat out of crude and the energy-inflation story, but it also drains haven demand, so gold gets little out of it. Longer term: the gap between Washington and Tehran on sanctions and Hormuz is still wide and escalation risk hasn't gone away; oil in the $100–$115 range keeps inflation and rates elevated.
