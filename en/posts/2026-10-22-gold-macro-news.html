Gold bounces off a near one-week low at 4,106 to 4,141 as yields ease, then slips back to 4,122 in early Asia – October hike odds down to 13.8%

FedNeutralOct 21, 2:12 PM ET (Reuters) · 7:05 PM ET (Fed Rate Monitor) · 7:36 PM ET (CNBC quote)

Reuters: spot gold was up 0.3% at $4,137.94 at 2:12 PM ET after touching its lowest level since Oct 15; December futures settled 0.2% higher at $4,150. Investing.com had spot settling at $4,141.28 (+0.4%). Investing.com's Fed Rate Monitor (7:05 PM ET Oct 21): 86.2% hold / 13.8% hike for Oct 28 (16.1% a day earlier, 33.3% a week ago); December shows 76.3% odds of a move to 4.00%–4.25%. Reuters, citing CME FedWatch: 88% odds of a December hike. Zaner Metals' Peter Grant sees gold range-bound with 'the bias to the downside'; UBS's Giovanni Staunovo says dips under 4,100 draw buyers but the upside stays capped while the Middle East conflict is live. CNBC XAU= at 7:36 PM ET: 4,122.04 (day range 4,106–4,141).

Gold impact: Near term: the market has all but priced out a hike next week, which gives gold room to breathe – but 4,141–4,162 is still where rallies get sold, and 4,100 is the floor to watch. Thursday's 8:30 AM ET jobless claims need to come in soft to dent December pricing. Longer term: as long as December hike odds sit above 75%, high real rates remain gold's main headwind.

Iran rebuffs U.S. demands via Pakistani mediators: no talks, no Hormuz reopening until Washington meets its conditions

War & geopoliticsNeutralOct 20 evening (Nournews) · Oct 21 session (Araghchi, new sanctions)

Reuters (via Arab News): Iran told Pakistan's army chief Asim Munir there will be no talks with Washington until its seven conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz are met – including an end to the U.S. port blockade, lifting oil sanctions and ending hostilities on all fronts; Supreme National Security Council secretary Mohsen Rezaei said all enriched uranium stays in Iran. Newsquawk: Foreign Minister Araghchi said the U.S. has shown no willingness to reach an understanding and there's little reason to expect progress. Washington rolled out new Iran-related sanctions while licensing the offload of non-Iranian cargo from some blocked vessels (a 30-day wind-down). The Houthis said talks with Saudi Arabia are deadlocked.

Gold impact: Near term: Tuesday's peace-deal headlines are dead – the risk premium is back in crude, which keeps inflation and yields elevated, so gold can't cash in on its haven bid; war headlines are good for $10–20 spikes, not trends. Longer term: only a real deal that reopens Hormuz knocks oil and hike pricing down – that's the biggest bullish catalyst for gold right now.

🔗 Source: Arab News (Reuters) – Iran to Pakistan's army chief: No talks with Washington before our demands are met · Newsquawk – Crude wrap: WTI settles at 97.15, Brent at 108.82

EIA: U.S. crude stocks draw 3.06 million barrels vs. 820,000 expected; Brent settles at $108.82, WTI at $97.15

OilBearish gold ↓Oct 21, 10:30 AM ET (EIA) · 2:30 PM ET (oil settlement)

CNBC: Brent added 3 cents to settle at $108.82 while WTI fell 67 cents to $97.15; Brent had been up as much as $2 to $110.85 after Iran played down any diplomatic progress. EIA (Reuters via BOE Report): crude inventories fell 3.06 million barrels to 412.5 million in the week to Oct 16 vs. a 820,000-barrel draw expected; gasoline −2.95 million, distillates +1.68 million (vs. −1.16 million expected). Goldman Sachs: average crude freight is running above $20 a barrel, roughly seven times last year's level.

Gold impact: Near term: crude is chopping in a 108–111 band – not low enough to cool inflation, so gold keeps trading off every oil swing. Longer term: Brent above $100 keeps inflation sticky and the Fed leaning hawkish, a headwind for gold.
