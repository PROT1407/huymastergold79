Gold lost about 4.1% this week, from 4,164.61 to a 3,994.65 close – its first trip under $4,000 since mid-July and its worst week since June. The root of it is crude: Houthi strikes on Saudi Arabia kept coming and Brent ran 6.2% on the week to $116.72, its highest settle since late April. That's showing up in the data – the PMI selling-price gauge rose at its fastest pace since August 2022 and UMich one-year inflation expectations hit 4.7% – so the market still has roughly 75% odds on a December hike. Here's the tell: the 10-year actually fell 11 bps on the week and gold still got hit. The AI selloff sent money into Treasuries, not bullion, and gold got sold to cover margin calls. When gold can't rally on lower yields, the bulls are in real trouble. My take: the 4,100–4,150 base is broken and $4,000 is now resistance. Next week I'm selling rallies into 4,028–4,040 and I'm not bottom-fishing before the Fed on Wednesday.
