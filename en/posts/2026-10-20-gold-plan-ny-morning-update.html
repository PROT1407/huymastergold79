The 4,100–4,106 buy zone never came into play. The 4,133–4,140 sell zone got run over by a string of green bars, with no red 45m or 1H close – so no short, and no stop to eat. That's a bounce with a real reason behind it, so I'm not going to stand in front of it. But the bigger trend hasn't flipped: the market still prices roughly 93% odds of a hike by December, and there's a wall overhead at 4,153–4,159.

Then the U.S. data gave the bulls a reason: housing starts at 1.22 million (1.30 million expected), then pending home sales down 7.3% against a 0.7% forecast, the weakest since at least 2001. A 7.5% mortgage rate is choking the most rate-sensitive corner of the economy – and the bond market heard it: the 10-year fell to 5.08%, the dollar dropped 0.4% to 101.5 and Brent slipped to $108. Gold did what the textbook says: it ran from 4,121 to 4,150 and filled the whole 4,133–4,142 gap. But one data point doesn't rewrite the story. The Fed is in its blackout ahead of the Oct. 27-28 meeting, the market still prices roughly 93% odds of a hike by December, and crude is still north of $100. My view: the rest of the New York session is a bounce running into resistance, not a reversal. The two levels that matter: 4,167 – a 1H close above it and the short is wrong; 4,119 – a 1H close below it and the data pop has failed. The job: sell 4,153–4,159 on a red close, buy a pullback into 4,130–4,136 on a green close, and sit on my hands in between.
