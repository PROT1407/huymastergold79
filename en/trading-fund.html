Building a trading fund

What we mean by a personal fund

A "fund" here is trading capital run with discipline: clear objectives, hard risk limits, a trade journal and regular reporting – instead of trading on gut feel. Job one isn't fast profits; it's protecting capital and showing the system has a positive expectancy over a meaningful sample.

Three pillars

  • System: written entry and exit rules, tested on historical data and then on a small live account.
  • Risk management: limits per trade, per day and per month.
  • Transparency: published results, ideally verified by a third party (Myfxbook, FXBlue, broker statements).

🛡 Basic dispute path — protect yourself

Goal: resolve fast, with evidence, without burning the bridge. Practical process — not a “how to attack the firm” guide.

  1. In-portal ticket / chat — include account ID, timestamps, the rule cited, dollar amount, and screenshots of dashboard/payout status. Ask for a written response window.
  2. Official support email (firm domain only) — same evidence pack; keep the thread. Skip random “admin” DMs.
  3. Evidence to keep: statements / MetriX / trade exports, KYC approval emails, IP/device screenshots if VPN is alleged, Master/PA agreement, fee invoices.
  4. Internal escalation: request Risk / Trust / Billing; stay factual and quote their published FAQ.
  5. Payment dispute / chargeback: last resort when the firm goes silent and you have proof the service wasn’t delivered — Topstep/Apex explicitly warn that bad-faith disputes can close accounts.
  6. Realistic expectations: 1–10+ business days of payout review is normal. “I passed, so the money is guaranteed” is the wrong model. Firms can deny under their TOS/FAQ.
