OilNeutral🕐 Oct 15, 3:06 PM ET (Reuters post-settlement update)

Crude splits at the settle: WTI jumps 3% to $97.97 on an 8.6M-barrel U.S. stock draw, Brent slips to $105.92 (−0.13%)

Reuters: Brent settled down 14 cents (−0.13%) at $105.92 a barrel, while WTI rose $2.88 (+3.03%) to $97.97. The EIA said U.S. crude stocks fell 8.6 million barrels to 415.6 million in the week ended Oct. 9, versus a 288,000-barrel draw expected in a Reuters poll. Brent was held back as Washington weighs easing sanctions on 'shadow fleet' tankers so legal oil can move on previously sanctioned vessels; the IEA agreed to release more than 20 million barrels of diesel as soon as possible, and Spain will tap 4.4 million barrels of reserves in October. Shipping attacks around Hormuz and the Saudi–Houthi conflict keep a geopolitical premium in the market.

Gold impact: Near term: Brent above $100 keeps energy inflation alive and supports Fed hike bets, neutral to slightly negative for gold; supply steps (IEA, sanctions relief) take some heat out. Longer term: if supply loosens, gasoline and diesel could cool into the Nov. 3 midterms, easing rate pressure and helping gold; a fresh Gulf strike would reverse that quickly.
