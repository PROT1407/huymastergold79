Gold climbs back toward $4,178 after Warsh offers no hike signal – December futures near $4,200, still down 0.5% on the week

CNBC (Reuters): spot gold rose 0.2% to $4,177.82/oz at 12:33 AM ET, with December futures up 0.7% at $4,199.10; bullion is still down 0.5% for the week. Gold had slipped as much as 0.6% to around $4,150–$4,160 early in Asia (Bloomberg) before a softer dollar after Warsh's remarks pulled it back up. OANDA's Kelvin Wong sees gold boxed in a $4,075–$4,221 range, with the bottom tested earlier this week, as September core CPI and core PPI both ran hot. CME FedWatch: 28% odds of an October hike and 89% for December. Yahoo Finance at 2:32 AM ET: GC=F futures at $4,199.40, up 0.7% from the $4,170.10 settle.

Gold impact: Near term: the dollar at a near two-week low and a neutral Warsh put $4,175–$4,200 back in play into London; $4,200–$4,221 (OANDA's range top, the 21-day SMA near $4,201 per FXStreet) has to give way decisively or sellers lean on it again. Longer term: hot US inflation and 89% December hike odds keep real-yield pressure on gold; Global X flags $4,000 as the line to watch if the Fed message turns hawkish.
