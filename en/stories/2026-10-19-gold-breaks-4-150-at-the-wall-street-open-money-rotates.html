Gold breaks $4,150 at the Wall Street open: money rotates back into AI stocks, the 10-year holds 5.10% – and a 29 NAHB print (31 expected) can't rescue it

CNBC live blog (10:19 AM ET): the Nasdaq rose 1.4% and the S&P 500 0.7% as investors bought the dip in AI names after last week's rout; Intel gained 5% on a JPMorgan upgrade. Reuters (10:01 AM ET): the NAHB/Wells Fargo builder sentiment index fell three points to 29 in October – the lowest since June 2012, versus a 31 forecast – as 7.40% mortgage rates and war-driven materials costs hit demand; 43% of builders cut prices. Kitco: markets price about 89% odds of a December Fed hike and roughly 15% for the Oct. 27-28 meeting. CNBC spot gold: 4,159–4,166 around the open (9:25–9:35 AM ET bars) → 4,152.70 (9:40 bar) → 4,146.00 low (9:45 bar) → back to 4,154.40 just before NAHB (10:00 AM ET) → 4,149.30 (10:00 bar) → 4,144.45 (10:10 AM ET bar) → 4,143.45 low (10:15 AM ET bar); gold-api.com 4,149.60 at 10:20 AM ET. 10-year 5.108%, DXY 101.90 at 10:10 AM ET.

Gold impact: Near term: no direct bad news – gold fell for lack of a reason to hold in a risk-on tape, and the break of the 4,159 London low tripped stops. A weak NAHB should have pulled yields lower, but the 10-year stayed at 5.10% – this market is trading inflation and the Fed, not housing. Longer term: as long as December hike odds sit near 89%, gold rallies are likely to get sold; softer jobs or inflation data is what would change the story.
