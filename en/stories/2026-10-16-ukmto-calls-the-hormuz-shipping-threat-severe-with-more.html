War & geopoliticsBullish for gold ↑🕐 Oct 15, 8:06 PM ET (UKMTO via Middle East Eye) · about 1:00 AM ET Oct 16 (Gulf News update)

UKMTO calls the Hormuz shipping threat 'severe' with more attacks 'highly likely' – Houthis hit Yemen's presidential palace, France deploys to Saudi Arabia

Middle East Eye (8:06 PM ET): UKMTO says the threat to merchant shipping in the Strait of Hormuz remains 'severe,' citing eight projectile attacks on four tankers and four container ships on Oct 11 and 13, including the GFS Pride, which caught fire and sank on Oct 14; there have been 149 maritime security incidents in the region since March 1. Gulf News (live, updated around 1:00 AM ET): a Houthi missile strike on the Al-Maashiq presidential palace in Aden killed at least four; Iran's army chief said Hormuz will stay 'closed,' which CENTCOM disputes; and President Trump told Newsmax he wouldn't use nuclear weapons and doesn't see US ground troops going into Iran. Arab News: France has begun deploying military assets in Saudi Arabia to protect oil facilities.

Gold impact: Near term: a wider conflict footprint (Hormuz, Yemen, Saudi Arabia) keeps haven demand alive, but it also props up the dollar, which limits the lift; Trump ruling out nukes and ground troops trims the extreme-escalation tail. Longer term: without a US–Iran deal, geopolitical risk stays a floor under gold, even though rates are the dominant driver right now.
