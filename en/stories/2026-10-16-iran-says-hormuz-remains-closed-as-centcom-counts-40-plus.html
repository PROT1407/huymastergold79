War & geopoliticsBullish for gold ↑🕐 Oct 15, 3:38 AM ET (CNN MOAB report) · 12:00 PM ET (Euronews) · through 7:31 PM ET (Gulf News live)

Iran says Hormuz 'remains closed' as CENTCOM counts 40-plus transits in 24 hours; another tanker burns in the Gulf of Oman – CNN: U.S. weighs MOAB

Gulf News (live, through 7:31 PM ET): Iran's army chief Maj. Gen. Amir Hatami said the Strait of Hormuz 'remains closed' until Iran's rights are respected; CENTCOM pushed back, saying U.S. and partner forces control the strait, more than 40 commercial vessels transited in the prior 24 hours and over 19 million barrels of crude moved through in a single day. Euronews (12:00 PM ET): an unnamed tanker caught fire in the Gulf of Oman after entering an Iranian-declared prohibited zone, the second such incident in 24 hours, following the sinking of GFS Pride and Cape Dao. CNN (via Gulf News) reported the U.S. is planning for possible use of the GBU-43/B MOAB against Iranian energy and nuclear sites if President Trump renews combat. Treasury Secretary Bessent announced new sanctions on Iran-linked financial networks, saying 'the noose is tightening.' Houthi missiles also targeted the presidential palace in Aden, Yemen.

Gold impact: Near term: the risk of renewed U.S. strikes on Iran and nonstop shipping attacks keep haven demand alive, which helps gold most when headlines hit by surprise; the same shocks lift crude and inflation expectations, which works against gold through rates. Longer term: a drawn-out, widening conflict (Saudi Arabia vs. the Houthis) keeps a floor under haven and central bank demand.
