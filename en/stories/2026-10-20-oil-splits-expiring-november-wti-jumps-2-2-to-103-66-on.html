Oil splits: expiring November WTI jumps 2.2% to $103.66 on Hormuz disruption, while Brent slips 0.3% to $109.55 on a 60-day Russia sanctions waiver

Reuters (via CNA): an oil tanker exploded after hitting a naval mine in the southern Strait of Hormuz on Sunday, according to the IRGC; Kpler says Gulf crude flows fell to about 8.5 million barrels per day last week from more than 20 million the week before, with at least 10 confirmed incidents involving commercial vessels. SEB expects Iran to escalate further ahead of the Nov. 3 U.S. midterms. Saudi Arabia issued danger alerts for Riyadh before lifting them, and a Houthi attack on a training camp in Yemen's Shabwa province killed 53. President Trump signed an order waiving some sanctions and tariffs on Russian exports after a diesel deal with Putin. DTN: November WTI settled at $103.66 (+2.22%), December Brent at $109.55 (−0.3%, session high $110.70).

Gold impact: Near term: high oil keeps inflation expectations and yields elevated – a headwind for gold through the Fed channel, even if war headlines spark $10–$15 haven pops. Longer term: a prolonged Hormuz squeeze forces the Fed to stay hawkish, and gold struggles to break out until war risk turns into growth risk.
