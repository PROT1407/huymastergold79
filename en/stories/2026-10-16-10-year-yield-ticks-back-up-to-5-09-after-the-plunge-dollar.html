10-year yield ticks back up to 5.09% after the plunge, dollar on track for its first weekly loss in five – Asian stocks bounce

Bloomberg (via swissinfo, 9:49 PM ET): Treasuries slipped across the curve after Thursday's haven rally, lifting the 10-year yield 4 bp to 5.09%; Japan's 10-year rose 5 bp to 2.94%. CNBC (Reuters): the dollar is headed for its first weekly decline in five weeks as AI-investment jitters and lower US yields stall its run – DXY at 102.07 (−0.14% on the week), EUR/USD at $1.1210, USD/JPY at 158.13. Asian equities rebounded: the Kospi rose about 3% after a near-8% plunge and the Nikkei gained 1.5–1.8%, while Indonesia fell 11% after MSCI flagged a possible downgrade. Yahoo Finance at 2:32 AM ET: DXY 101.86 (−0.19%).

Gold impact: Near term: a modest yield bounce is a drag, but the weaker dollar offsets it, so gold mostly grinds sideways to higher; if the 10-year pushes back above 5.10% in London or New York, the bounce likely stalls. Longer term: a 10-year near 5% keeps the opportunity cost of holding gold high, while AI-debt worries could keep pushing money into havens.
