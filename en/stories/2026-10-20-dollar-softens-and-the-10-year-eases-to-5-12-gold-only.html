Dollar softens and the 10-year eases to 5.12% – gold only reaches $4,145, then gets sold back to $4,118 before the European open

CNBC (Reuters): spot gold rose 0.1% to $4,145.22 at 11:47 PM ET on a softer dollar; CME FedWatch shows 82% odds of a hold on Oct. 28 but 89% for a December hike. Capital.com's Kyle Rodda expects a big move whatever the Fed does, with the initial reaction possibly supportive for gold on a hold. BOJ Governor Kazuo Ueda said underlying inflation is 'quite close' to the 2% target, with global AI demand adding price pressure – leaving room for more hikes. By 1:30 AM ET gold was back at $4,132.15 (Economy Middle East); CNBC XAU= shows a $4,118.40 low at 2:15 AM ET and $4,134.40 at 2:50 AM ET. CNBC: DXY 101.77 (−0.20%), 10-year 5.119% (−1.7 bp).

Gold impact: Near term: when a weaker dollar and lower yields can't hold a gold rally, sellers are in control – bounces into $4,140–$4,150 are likely to get sold. Longer term: December Fed hike pricing and a hawkish-leaning BOJ keep global yields elevated, gold's main headwind until inflation cools.
