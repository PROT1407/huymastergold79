OilNeutral🕐 Oct 18, 9:16 PM ET (CNBC Daily Open) → CNBC update overnight → quote 2:43 AM ET

Oil drops more than 2% to about $107 as Trump waives sanctions and tariffs on Russian energy and touts 'significant progress' on Ukraine – even after Kyiv's 1,000-drone weekend strike

CNBC: crude fell Monday on expectations of more Russian supply after Friday's U.S. sanctions relief – President Trump invoked national interest to suspend new duties on buyers of Russian energy and lifted sanctions on Russian diesel exports. Brent fell 1.8% to $107.88 and WTI 1.14% to $100.25; CNBC quoted Brent at $107.28 (−2.35%) at 2:43 AM ET. CNBC Daily Open (Sunday, 9:16 PM ET): Ukraine launched more than 1,000 drones across Russia over the weekend, setting a St. Petersburg oil terminal and depot ablaze; Zelenskyy called the U.S. decision 'wrong,' and Treasury Secretary Bessent told him to 'be careful.' Per Newsquawk, Trump posted on Truth Social that Russia–Ukraine peace talks are making 'significant progress.' The Gulf is still hot: two more tankers were attacked off Saudi Arabia on Friday (UKMTO).

Gold impact: Near term: cheaper oil cools inflation fears and yields, cushioning gold around $4,160–$4,170, but peace headlines drain the haven bid, so don't expect a big pop. Longer term: if Russian barrels really come back, oil and rate-hike expectations fall together – good for gold. A Hormuz attack can still send Brent back to $110–$120 at any moment.
