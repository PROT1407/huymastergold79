Cross-assetBullish for gold ↑🕐 Oct 15, 4:48 PM ET (Reuters update)

Dollar backs off an 18-month high: DXY −0.45% to 101.99, euro posts its best day in nearly two months – a tailwind for gold

Reuters (4:48 PM ET): the dollar index fell 0.45% to 101.99 after hitting an 18-month high on Wednesday; the euro rose 0.56% to $1.1218, sterling gained 0.64% to $1.3275, the yen firmed 0.15% to 158.08 per dollar, and the Aussie lost 0.77% after a surprise drop in Australian employment. The bond rally and cautious Fed remarks (Bowman, Daly) cooled October hike bets; CME FedWatch (per Reuters) showed a 73% chance of a hold at the Oct. 27–28 meeting, up from 53% a month ago. Monex USA's Juan Perez said the market has turned 'anti-USD' after four weeks of heavy dollar gains.

Gold impact: Near term: a weaker dollar makes bullion cheaper for non-U.S. buyers, which supports price. Longer term: most major brokerages still see one more Fed hike in December (Reuters), so the dollar can regain its footing if inflation stays hot – a risk for gold.
