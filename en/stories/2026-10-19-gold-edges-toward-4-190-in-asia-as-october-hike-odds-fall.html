Gold edges toward $4,190 in Asia as October hike odds fall below 20% – December still ~92%; gold ETFs log a 13th straight week of inflows

Reuters (Sunday, 8:31 PM ET): spot gold was up 0.2% at $4,174.54/oz and December futures up 0.4% at $4,195.70. CME FedWatch puts the odds of an October hike at 16%, but a December hike at 92%. U.S. import prices rose more than expected in September on energy – more evidence inflation pressure is building. The IMF expects the ECB to hike by 25 bps at least once more this year. Bloomberg (updated 12:21 AM ET Monday): gold rose as much as 0.5% to near $4,190, erasing most of last week's loss, after traders read Chair Warsh's remarks as backing a hold next week; gold ETFs have added holdings for 13 straight weeks, though bullion is still down about a fifth since the U.S.-Iran war broke out in late February. CNBC spot (XAU=) at 2:50 AM ET: 4,178.30.

Gold impact: Near term: fading October hike bets are why gold is holding above $4,160 – but with December still ~92% priced, it's hard to crack the $4,198–$4,208 lid without a fresh catalyst. Longer term: 13 straight weeks of ETF inflows say big money is still buying the dips, building a floor around $4,000–$4,100.
